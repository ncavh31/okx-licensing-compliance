## The US record: a guilty plea, $504 million, and a platform that's barely a year and a half old

On 24 February 2025, Aux Cayes FinTech Co. Ltd., OKX's global operating company, pleaded guilty in the Southern District of New York to operating an unlicensed money transmitting business while serving US customers. The number that gets quoted is "over $500 million," which hides the structure. It breaks down as an **$84 million fine plus forfeiture of about $421 million in fees collected from US customers**, totalling more than $504 million. OKX must retain an external compliance consultant through February 2027. The acting US Attorney said the compliance failures led to more than $5 billion in suspicious transactions. OKX's position was that there were "no allegations of customer harm."

Both things are true at once: nobody has alleged that OKX lost customer funds in this matter, and the company paid half a billion dollars for letting US customers onto a platform that wasn't licensed to serve them.

The practical consequence for Americans is the relaunch timeline. OKX came back to the US in April 2025 through OKX US, headquartered in San Jose. If you're American, you're on a platform in the early stage of its second compliance attempt, not a long-settled domestic exchange with a decade of US regulatory history behind it. That's a difference in track record, not in licence status.

## Has OKX ever been hacked?

No known hack has taken customer funds at the exchange level. That's the accurate version of a claim you'll see stated more strongly elsewhere. Two incidents still belong in the answer.

In June 2024, some user accounts were drained. SlowMist's analysis said the attacker created new API keys and withdrew funds. OKX's account was that someone forged judicial documents to obtain information on a very small number of customers, that 2FA could not be bypassed, and that affected users were reimbursed. Take the reimbursement as the part that matters to you.

In March 2025, OKX paused its Web3 DEX aggregator, saying the Lazarus Group was attempting to misuse its DeFi services in connection with the roughly $1.5 billion Bybit theft. Bloomberg reported around $100 million had moved through OKX; OKX did not confirm that figure. OKX said it froze related funds that reached the exchange and that no OKX user funds were lost.

The pattern in both cases: account-level and adjacent-service risk, not a break in the exchange's custody. If you hold large balances, that distinction doesn't help you much. Enable 2FA with a hardware key or authenticator app, set anti-phishing codes, whitelist withdrawal addresses, and keep the bulk of long-term holdings off any exchange.

## Malta fined the European arm, and the terms say you're not insured

In April 2025, Malta's Financial Intelligence Analysis Unit fined OKCoin Europe about **€1.05 million (roughly $1.2 million)** over failures to properly assess money laundering and terrorist financing risks tied to its products. The FIAU noted improvements made over the previous 18 months, so this reads as a supervisory penalty rather than an existential finding. Some outlets round it to €1.1 million or describe it as "about €1 million"; the FIAU's own figure is the one to use.

Then there's the insurance question, which is where a lot of "is OKX safe" content goes wrong. OKX's own security page describes a security fund intended to cover losses from system failures and liquidations, gives no fund size, and states that the fund is not government-insured. Separately, OKX's terms are explicit that accounts are not insured and that in a shortfall scenario assets may not be recoverable in full on short notice. The US disclosures say the same in regulatory language: virtual currency is not legal tender, isn't backed by the US government, and balances are not covered by FDIC or SIPC protection.

So: OKX operates a compensation scheme on its own terms, and your account is not insured. Both sentences are needed. In the EU, MiCA does impose safeguarding and custody rules that give you a regulator to complain to, which is a real difference from the Seychelles entity, but it still isn't deposit insurance.

## FAQ

**Is OKX legal in the US?**
Yes, through OKX INC., which is FinCEN-registered as a money services business and holds state money transmitter licences. It does not serve New York, American Samoa, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands or the US Virgin Islands. Using a VPN to reach the global platform violates OKX's terms.

**Does OKX have insurance?**
No. OKX describes a security fund for losses from system failures and liquidations, and states that the fund isn't government-insured. Its terms say accounts aren't insured and that a shortfall may not be recoverable in full at short notice.

**Has OKX ever been hacked?**
No known exchange-level hack of customer funds. There was account-level theft in June 2024 via newly created API keys, with affected users reimbursed, and OKX paused its Web3 DEX aggregator in March 2025 over Lazarus Group activity. No OKX user funds were lost in the latter.

**Is OKX available in the UK?**
Yes, but narrowly. UK accounts are held with the Seychelles entity, no FCA registration is named, and you get spot trading and conversion only, with no derivatives, P2P, API access, trading bots or GBP deposits.

**Is OKX safe for beginners?**
It's functional and heavily featured, which is the problem. The interface assumes you know what an order book and a stop-loss are. Start with spot only, enable 2FA before your first deposit, and don't touch derivatives until you understand liquidation.
